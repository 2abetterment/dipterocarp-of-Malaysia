Description
The Dipterocarpaceae are the most ecologically dominant family of trees in the lowland and hill rainforests of Peninsular Malaysia, forming a conspicuous emergent layer 40–70 m above the forest floor. The family comprises approximately 680–700 species worldwide, the great majority concentrated in the Malesian region.
Diagnostic Characters
Trees, rarely shrubs; resin canals present in bark and wood. Leaves simple, alternate, stipules caducous. Flowers bisexual, actinomorphic, 5-merous; petals contorted in bud; stamens 5–150; ovary superior, 3-locular. Fruit a 1-seeded indehiscent nut with 2–5 enlarged persistent calyx wings.

Distribution and Habitat
Dipterocarps dominate Mixed Dipterocarp Forest (MDF) from sea level to 1,200 m. The family exhibits supra-annual community-level mast flowering triggered by El Niño drought, occurring every 3–7 years. Seeds are recalcitrant with very short viability.
