Order Malvales · Family

Dipterocarpaceae Blume, 1825

Bijdr. fl. Ned. Ind. 13: 222 (1825)

Trees, rarely shrubs, with resinous or balsamic secretions. Leaves simple, alternate, stipulate. Flowers bisexual, 5-merous. Fruit a 1-seeded nut subtended or enclosed by the enlarged, wing-like calyx lobes — the diagnostic character from which the family takes its name: di-pteron-karpos, two-winged fruit.

Diagnostic characters

Habit
Tabiat
Large trees, often emergent, reaching 70 m; bole straight and cylindrical, frequently with plank buttresses at the base.
Bark & Resin
Kulit kayu & Resin
Bark fissured or scaly, inner bark often laminated and reddish. All species possess resin canals secreting aromatic resins (damar); some produce camphor.
Leaves
Daun
Simple, alternate, coriaceous; stipules present, often large and caducous leaving conspicuous scars. Secondary venation pinnate; domatia sometimes present in vein axils.
Flowers
Bunga
Bisexual, actinomorphic, 5-merous; petals contorted in bud, usually white to cream or pink; stamens 5 to many, anthers with a prolonged connective appendage.
Fruit
Buah
1-seeded nut; calyx lobes persistent and enlarging into wings (0, 2, 3, or 5) which aid wind dispersal. Wing number and size are primary characters for genus identification.
Timber
Kayu balak
The most commercially important timber family in Malaysia. Trade names — Meranti, Keruing, Kapur, Balau, Selangan Batu — each encompass multiple species grouped by density and working properties.
Ecology

Canopy dominants

Dipterocarps form the emergent and main canopy layers of lowland and hill mixed dipterocarp forest — the signature forest type of Sundaland and the richest in the world by timber volume.

Timber

Meranti, Keruing, Balau

The family supplies the majority of Malaysia's commercial hardwood export. Species are grouped into trade classes by density: light, medium and heavy hardwood.

Conservation

Under pressure

Slow growth rates (30–80 years to maturity) and high timber value have placed most species on the IUCN Red List. Habitat loss remains the primary threat across the region.

13 genera in Peninsular Malaysia

Thirteen genera are recorded in Peninsular Malaysia, out of 16 genera worldwide. Genera are distinguished primarily by the number and relative size of calyx wings on the fruit. Upuna is confined to Sarawak and is not included here.

Ashton (2014) recognised additional segregate genera from Shorea — Anthoshorea, Richetia and Rubroshorea — now accepted by POWO.
